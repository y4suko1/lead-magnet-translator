**IDEA 1**

**Title:** The "Say It Out Loud" Pricing Script

**Format:** A one-page printable script, three short scenarios (quoting live, following a shaky moment, handling a pause after the number)

**Core Problem:** People rehearse their pricing structure endlessly but never rehearse the actual sentence that comes out of their mouth. One attendee asked outright for the actual words, as a script.

**Microsolution:** Three ready-to-say scripts for the moment someone asks "what's the investment," so the reader has words in their mouth before they need them, not just a number in their head.

**Why This Converts:** This is the clearest, most specific ask in the session: one attendee asked for a script, and another described freezing at the same moment. An ask someone made out loud is the lead magnet, not a guess at one.

**Pitch Line:** "You already know your price. This gives you the exact words to say it without flinching."

**IDEA 2**

**Title:** The Confident Pause Cheat Sheet

**Format:** A short checklist plus a printable card people can keep next to their phone during calls

**Core Problem:** The host's point was that clients notice tone more than the number itself. A wavering voice invites the negotiation, and the host said a steady tone after the number does more work than any discount.

**Microsolution:** A simple two-step move (say the number, then hold a steady tone and a short pause) that replaces the instinct to soften the number as you say it.

**Why This Converts:** This builds on the host's point that clients notice tone more than the price itself. An idea built on a genuine "aha" moment gets shared and talked about, not just downloaded and forgotten.

**Pitch Line:** "It's not your price that scares clients off. It's what your voice does right after you say it."

**Source:** "It's not the number, it's your tone when you say it. If your voice wavers, they notice that more than the price itself." (line 65) · "Right, the wavering invites the negotiation. **IDEA 3**

**Title:** The Pushback Story Reality Check

**Format:** A short quiz, five questions, ends with a one-line reframe for the reader's specific pricing fear

**Core Problem:** One attendee assumed clients were mentally negotiating them down before the conversation even started, an assumption the host pointed out usually isn't even true.

**Microsolution:** A quick way to separate what a reader is actually afraid will happen on a call from what usually really happens, so they walk away with one clear, personal reframe instead of a generic pep talk.

**Why This Converts:** This targets the emotional block sitting underneath the pricing conversation, not the pricing conversation itself. A quiz that hands someone their own specific fear back to them, named clearly, is far more shareable than a generic tips list.

**Pitch Line:** "Find out what you're actually afraid will happen when you say your price, and why it probably won't."

**Source:** "A lot of you are also carrying a story that if you say the number confidently, the client will push back or leave." (line 46) · "Constantly. I assume every client is doing math in their head about how to talk me down." (line 50)
